# Market feasibility and business planning (working hypotheses)

_Compiled from the Bridge planning discussions; figures below are illustrative and should be reverified before any investment/launch decision._

## Market map
Random chat/roulette, friend discovery, language-specific audio communities and pen-pal services already have large competitors including Litmatch, FRND, Slowly, Bottled, RandoChat and Azar. Basic matching, mood-based chat, interest filters, voice parties and gifts are **not novel inventions**.

## Opportunity hypothesis
Better conversation completion and safety; less spam and intrusive advertising; frictionless mutual reconnect; unified Quick Chat and Topic Match experience. Competitive advantage would come from demonstrated execution and trusted community, not feature-name novelty.

## Biggest risks
- **Liquidity/cold start**: same-time compatible users, not install count. Validate live concurrency and wait-time distributions before opening many pools.
- **Trust/safety**: privacy, spam, harassment, legal exposure, moderation response and high-severity incidents.
- **Unit economics**: paid conversion and ad revenue may be outweighed by acquisition, infra and moderation spend.
- **App-store acceptance**: especially iOS random/anonymous-chat guidance. No approval is guaranteed.
- **Localization at scale**: each supported language needs actual safety support; neutral product policy does not mean unverified worldwide operational coverage.

## Suggested business model (to test)
Free matching and basic conversations. Optional subscription around ₹99–₹199/month, cosmetic identity/theme purchases, limited ads only outside an active conversation. Never sell personal message data or withhold basic safety.

## Example subscription math
At 25,000 MAU, 1.5% subscribers at ₹149/month implies 375 subscribers, ₹55,875 gross subscription purchases per month (before taxes/fees/refunds). This is **not** profit or a forecast. Ad RPM, conversion, payment deductions, moderation hours and retention are unverified variables.

## Go/no-go research
Interview adult users; test onboarding, actual match wait-times, first conversation satisfaction, D7 retention, mutual connection rate and revenue/operating cost with transparent instrumentation. Pilot selection must not encode preferential ranking of supported languages.
